CIRCULAR ECONOMY: DEVELOPED AND DEVELOPING COUNTRIES PERSPECTIVES The Dutch government has implemented a circular economy system with a vision extending up to 2050, structured around the R10 framework (Refuse, Rethink, Reduce, Reuse, Repair, Refurbish, Remanufacture, Repurpose, Recycle, and Recover Energy). This model, which practically excludes landfills from the circular economy cycle, is particularly well-suited for developed countries, like the Netherlands. In developing countries where both, the construction of new Sanitary Landfills working as Biodigesters and the required social inclusion of the people that now make a living as waste pickers in the thousands of open landfills now being closed in Brazil, are relevant components of Solid Waste Systems. The ERASMUS+ EGALITARIAN Project aims to explore the potential for adapting the Dutch R10 Model to create a version suitable for developing countries, specifically targeting contexts like Brazil and other developing countries. Initially, the project proposes the development of a modified model, named the P3RD Model: P= Prevention; R1= Reduction; R2= Reuse; R3= Recycling; and D= Disposal. Besides considering that both, the Solid Waste Disposal into Biodigester Sanitary Landfills and the social-economical inclusion of the waste pickers now making a living in the existing open dumpsites, are relevant elements, this modification simplifies the original framework by focusing on five titles, rather than the ten utilized in the Dutch R10 Model. This adaptation seeks to establish circular economy project management guides tailored to the needs and capacities of developing nations. It is worthwhile to emphasize that all Rs of the Dutch R10 Model are included in the five titles of the P3RD Model: (i) Prevention and Reduction via Product Engineering (PR1) include Refuse, Rethink, Reduce; (ii) Reuse (R2) encompasses Reuse, Repair, Refurbish, Remanufacture, Repurpose; (iii) Recycling (R3) that includes only Recycling; and (iv) Disposal (D) into Biodigester Sanitary Landfills, built in such a way to avoid land contamination and allowing industrial use of the methane gas generated. As shown in Figure 1, the process used by Cooperatives of Waste Pickers in Brasilia, for both, R2-Reuse and R3-Recycling can be described in terms of Selective Collection→C1, Triage→T2, Transformation→T2 (Material: Biological, Physical, Chemical; and Organizational: Environmental, Cooperativism and Education; Decent Work Conditions; Creation of Cooperatives); and Commercialization→C2. Regarding Environmental Education in Organizational Transformation, a basic premise is that the inclusion and representation of waste pickers in educational and awareness initiatives are relevant issues for fostering sustainable waste management practices. A workshop was then held to develop AI-generated personas to be used in an App that authentically represent waste pickers and serve as educators for the external community on proper waste separation and collection. The workshop involved ten waste pickers (seven women and three men) belonging to CENTCOOP, an entity that represents more than twenty cooperatives of waste pickers, jointly conducted by students from AAU, Saxion University and UnB, under the supervision of UnB professors. Using an AI-powered program developed for Educado, an Egalitarian Erasmus+ Project, iteratively generated images based on their input, collecting feedback until they were satisfied with the final personas, one female and one male. Concurrently, participants identified their main struggles in waste collection, reaching a consensus on contamination as the biggest challenge. They elaborated on specific problematic materials, including hospital waste, low-quality recyclables, organic matter, and even animal corpses. Additionally, they shared their expectations for systemic improvements, highlighting the need for fines, external education, and enhanced awareness campaigns. Notably, they requested AI-generated images of children to serve as influencers in educating younger generations. The results demonstrated a strong sense of ownership and engagement among the participants, who expressed satisfaction with the personas, often comparing them to real individuals within

BRAZILIAN TAX INCENTIVE FOR REUSABLE AND RECYCLABLE MATERIALS The Decree No. 12.106, of July 10,2024, that regulates the Law No. 14.260, of December 8, 2021, is an example of public policy regarding Circular Economy in Brazil, with a focus on tax incentives associated with reusable and recyclable materials. It follows a free translation from Portuguese to English of the Decree No. 12.106. “Article 1 The tax incentive for the recycling production chain established in Law No. 14,260, of December 8, 2021, is regulated, with a view to promoting the use of raw materials and inputs from recyclable and recycled materials. Article 2 Individuals and legal entities taxed on the basis of actual profit may deduct part of the income tax due to direct support to projects previously approved by the Ministry of Environment and Climate Change, aimed at:

I - capacitation, training and technical advice, including for the promotion of national and international exchanges, for the school and academic areas, business, community associations and social organizations that explicitly include the promotion, development, execution or support of recycling or reuse activities for materials,

II - incubation of micro-enterprises, small enterprises, cooperatives and solidarity social enterprises that operate in recycling activities,

III - research and studies to support actions that involve shared responsibility for the life cycle of products,

IV - implementation and adaptation of physical infrastructure of micro-enterprises, small enterprises, industries, cooperatives and associations of collectors of reusable and recyclable materials,

V - acquisition of equipment and vehicles for the selective collection, reuse, processing, treatment and recycling of materials by industries, micro-enterprises, small enterprises, cooperatives and associations of collectors of reusable and recyclable materials,

VI - organization and support for marketing networks and production chains, integrated by micro-enterprises, small enterprises, cooperatives and associations of collectors of reusable and recyclable materials,

VII - strengthening the participation of collectors of reusable and recyclable materials in recycling chains; and

VIII - Development of new technologies to add value to the work of collecting reusable and recyclable materials.
