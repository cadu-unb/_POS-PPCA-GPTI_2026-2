# P3D — Lessons learned in the PMBOK 8 synthesis

**SIPA Privacy 2 group**

## Decisions that improved the work

**1. Keep principles, Focus Areas, domains, and processes distinct.** These four elements answer different questions: a principle guides judgment; a Focus Area locates an activity type; a domain brings together a management concern; a process describes a possible practice. An early visual map risked suggesting a rigid chain between them. Different verbs on the arrows and a 7 × 5 matrix made their relationships visible without implying equivalence.

**2. Check all 40 processes against the primary source.** Text extracted from the PDF loses the column position of some Table 2-1 entries. Reading the original page confirmed that *Identify Stakeholders* belongs to Initiating. The Focus Area totals are therefore 2, 19, 8, 10, and 1; domain totals are 9, 6, 3, 4, 7, 5, and 6. Recording the source of each count makes the map auditable.

**3. Make tailoring the organizing thread.** A list of 40 names alone explains little. Chapter 3 of the *Guide* shows why and how to adjust the life cycle, processes, and engagement. We began to read each domain by asking: what tailoring decision does this domain require in the project context? This connects the technical inventory with the *Standard*'s emphasis on value.

**4. Separate PMBOK 8 from our analytical grouping.** Four concentrations help present the seven domains, but they are not an official PMI classification. SIPA themes are possible additions and do not enter the 40-process total. Making this boundary explicit in captions and prose prevents incorrect attribution.

**5. Use figures to explain, not decorate.** The heat map shows distribution, the cycle represents decision review, and the concentration cards separate official content from the analytical grouping. Each figure needs a title, source, key, and interpretive sentence. Color supports reading, while numbers and labels remain usable without it.
