# P3A — Lessons learned

**SIPA Privacy 2 group · P3A assignment**

## What we learned while developing the proposal

**1. A precise gap statement improved the argument.** An initial hypothesis said that PMBOK 8 addressed privacy only implicitly. Reading the guide revealed explicit references to data protection, GDPR, and privacy, including in its discussion of artificial intelligence. The defensible claim is narrower: the seven official domains and 40 processes do not include a domain and process set dedicated to managing the effects of personal-data processing on individuals. This correction prevents the extension from relying on an absolute absence that the source does not support.

**2. Stakeholders and data subjects overlap but are not identical.** Stakeholders processes help identify people and organize communication and engagement. A person may nevertheless have their data processed without taking part in the project or knowing it exists. The proposed domain must make these data subjects and data operations visible while retaining clear interfaces with existing processes.

**3. Risk to the project does not exhaust risk to the person.** The Risk domain supplied a useful structure for analysis, responses, and monitoring. NIST references helped distinguish harm arising from data processing itself from cybersecurity incidents. We therefore connect each assessment to affected people, purpose, and decisions rather than creating a parallel system that ignores the general risk register.

**4. Assignment ambiguity required an explicit editorial choice.** The general P3A description mentions all four SIPA domains, while the submission paragraph mentions one domain per group. To produce a reviewable contribution, we developed **Privacy → Who** and included Sustainability, Innovation, and Business Analysis only in an integration map. We also analyzed alignment with the six principles, although the supporting course text reserves their full integration for P3C. These choices should remain visible during collective review.

**5. The recycling case made the proposal testable.** The data path described in P2B, from digital mobilization through cooperative recycling to final disposal, raised concrete questions about identifying data subjects, limiting sharing, and assigning responsibility for records at the end of their life cycle. It is a project scenario, not evidence that an actual cooperative is legally compliant.

## What we would revise next

We would test the eight processes with project, privacy, and cooperative practitioners to remove duplication and calibrate the effort required in settings with little or extensive personal data. For each process, we would verify that inputs are available, a decision has an owner, the output is useful, and the interface with Stakeholders and Risk is clear. We would bring the other-concentration links to P3C as proposals rather than presenting unapproved changes as final results.

**Limit of this submission:** the matrices and processes are a documentary academic proposal. They are not an official PMI extension, a legal audit, or empirical validation.
